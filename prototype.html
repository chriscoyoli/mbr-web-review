Membership

Join the Monterey Bay Region

Membership is through Porsche Club of America, and you choose your home region when you sign up. Choose Monterey Bay and you are on the tour list, the dinner list, and the concours field on the Central Coast.

Join at PCA National Come to an event first

Joining takes about five minutes on pca.org. You will be asked for your car’s VIN and your home region. Our region code is MBY.

Richard Chew

What you actually get here

Twenty or so region events a year

Driving tours on Highway 1 and through Carmel Valley, the Concours in Paradise every July, tech sessions, and a membership dinner on the first Monday of most months.

Zone 7 and National on top

Nine neighbouring regions run their own calendars, and the Zone 7 concours and autocross series are open to you. Treffen and Parade are national.

The people who know these cars

Four hundred owners locally, and the club’s national technical resources. Somebody in this region has already solved whatever your car is doing.

Panorama, every month

PCA’s national magazine arrives with membership, along with the members-only classifieds at The Mart.

Dual region, if you split your time

Plenty of our members belong to a second region as well. If you are half here and half somewhere else, you can have both.

Family membership included

One membership covers an affiliate member in the same household, which matters because most of our events are the kind you bring somebody to.

Current PCA National dues are set by National and change from time to time. Check pca.org for today’s figure

Not sure yet?

Come to something first. Nearly everything on our calendar welcomes guests, and nobody is going to ask what you drive at the door. The membership meeting on the first Monday of the month is the easiest place to start, and the tours are the fastest way to meet people.

If you would rather ask a question before you turn up, our membership chair reads every email.

About the region

More than four hundred members across Monterey and San Benito Counties, one of 149 regions of the Porsche Club of America and one of ten regions in Zone 7.

Richard Chew

Whether you drive a vintage 356, a daily-driver Boxster, or something with a roll cage in it, you are welcome here. The club has never been about what is in the garage.

We run driving tours along the Central Coast, from a half-day loop through Carmel Valley to long weekends down Highway 1 and up into the Santa Cruz Mountains. Our Concours in Paradise takes over Carmel Valley Community Park every July and draws around a hundred cars. Members compete in the Zone 7 autocross series at Salinas Municipal Airport. In between there are tech sessions in the shops of the people who keep these cars running, cars and coffee mornings, and a membership dinner on the first Monday of most months.

The club we belong to

The Porsche Club of America is the largest single-marque car club in the world, with more than 170,000 members. Membership includes Panorama magazine every month, the members-only classifieds at The Mart, and the club’s technical resources. Membership records and dues are held by PCA National, not by the region.

Where we came from

The region was chartered in 1961 and has had a president in every year since. For two decades the club published The Montereyan, a monthly newsletter that ran from 1975 to 1997 and is now one of the better records of Central Coast car culture in that period. The full archive is online.

2026 Concours in Paradise

24 July 2026 · Vega Roecker · 4 minute read

Richard Chew

The field at Carmel Valley Community Park, Sunday 19 July.

Around a hundred cars came to Carmel Valley Community Park on 19 July for the region’s longest-running event, and for once the marine layer burned off before judging started.

Concours in Paradise has been on the Monterey Bay calendar for decades, and the shape of it has not changed much: the field opens early, the cars come in over the grass, and by nine there is a line of them under the oaks with owners going over paintwork with a detailing cloth for the fourth time. Placeholder body copy

The whole point of a concours in a club like this one is that the judging is done by members, on cars owned by members, in front of the people who will be driving those cars home afterwards. Nobody trailers anything in. Several of the entries had been on the Springtime Sojourn in April, which is a good sign.

Behind the prototype

Design rationale

Eleven screens, built to be achievable inside the existing WordPress site rather than to win a design award. Every layout decision below traces back to a specific finding in the audit.

The one decision everything else follows from

On the current site a phone user scrolls 3.9 screens before seeing a single event. Measured on this prototype, “What’s on next” sits 0.82 screens down at 390 pixels, 0.96 at 768, and 0.61 at 1440, so it is on the first screen at every width. The whole home page is 4,420 pixels tall on a phone against the current 6,330. That comes from making the hero a text block, keeping the photograph to a shallow band until there is room to set it beside the words, and putting nothing else in front of the events. Filling events is priority one for this club, so events get the position that matters.

Status is information, not decoration

Every event carries a coloured rail and a status chip: places available, nearly full, sold out, cancelled. This is the direct fix for the audit's most serious content finding, a cancelled event still being advertised as upcoming. Events Manager Pro already holds the booking counts. Once the display reads them, that mistake becomes structurally impossible rather than a thing someone has to remember.

Colour

A deep marine indigo carried over from the club's existing header, a warm sandstone paper rather than plain white, and one accent: a burnt amber taken from Carmel sandstone in late light and from the flag stands at a circuit. Amber is the only accent, used for calls to action, links, and section eyebrows. The four status colours are semantic and deliberately separate from it, so a green chip never reads as brand and an amber button never reads as a warning.

Type

Source Serif 4 for headings, at a wide range of sizes, doing the work that a decorative brand font would otherwise have to do. Public Sans for everything else, including numbers, which are set with tabular figures so that prices, dates and seat counts line up. Two families, not three, because the site should not be downloading fonts it does not need.

Photography

Every photograph is a real one from the region's own media library, credited to the member who took it: Fred Brown on the Big Sur banner, Richard Chew at Taste Morgan and the concours, Vega Roecker at Elkhorn Slough and the membership meeting. No stock imagery anywhere. Three slots stay as labelled placeholders because no real asset exists yet: the four board portraits, the meeting-point map, and a webstore product shot. Those are a shot list, not a design decision.

Trademarks

No Porsche crest, no Porsche wordmark, no shield shape that could be mistaken for either. The mark is the region's own crest, supplied by the board and used as supplied: a headland, the bay, an oak, and a red car on a white road, over the letters PCA. It depends on no licence the region does not already hold. "Porsche Club of America" appears as the name of the organisation, which is what a chartered region is licensed to do. The footer carries an explicit non-affiliation notice modelled on the one BMW CCA chapters use.

Accessible and fast by construction

  • One h1 per screen, headings in order, and no empty headings. The audit found two h1 elements on the current home page, one of them blank.
  • Every standalone control clears the 24 pixel WCAG 2.2 minimum: buttons, card links, menu items and footer links included, with primary buttons at 48 pixels. The only targets under 24 pixels are links inside running sentences, which the standard explicitly exempts. The current site misses this on 33 targets.
  • Every link and control has a visible text label. Focus is a solid three-pixel amber outline, never removed.
  • Form fields have real labels, required fields are marked in text as well as colour, and the consent checkbox is explicit.
  • Both light and dark themes are defined at token level and tested in all three states: explicit light, explicit dark, and the system default.
  • No framework, no animation library, no icon font. Two web fonts, a single stylesheet, and about thirty lines of JavaScript that exist only to run the prototype's screen switcher.
  • prefers-reduced-motion is respected.

What this prototype deliberately does not do

No parallax, no scroll animation, no hero video, no member login, no on-site photo galleries, no forum, no app. Each of those was considered and rejected in the audit as either expensive to build, expensive to maintain, or already solved elsewhere. The point of Option A is a site one volunteer can run.

Written for the recommended path, which is the existing WordPress install, the existing bootscore theme with a child theme, and Events Manager Pro left exactly where it is.

How the store works once WooCommerce is gone

The review recommends retiring the self-hosted shop. This is what replaces it, in enough detail to actually set up.

Clothing goes to the national webstore

Email webstore@national.pca.org to open a Monterey Bay storefront. PCA prints to order in Columbia, Maryland and posts direct to the member. The region supplies its logo once and then does nothing: no stock, no money fronted, no fulfilment, no returns, and no question about who is licensed to put the club’s marks on a shirt. The Regalia page links out to it.

Badges are sold with one Stripe payment link

A name badge is club-specific and nobody else can make it, so it stays on the site. One Stripe Payment Link, priced at $15, with adjustable quantity and three custom fields: Name as it should appear (required, 24 characters), Second line (optional), and Anything else. Stripe hosts the payment page and emails the receipt. The Regalia and Badge pages link straight to it.

What the volunteer actually does

  1. Watch orders come in. Stripe emails a notification per payment. No plugin, no admin login on the website.
  2. Run a batch. In the Stripe dashboard, filter Payments by the badge product, then export to CSV. The custom-field columns are the engraving list, already spelled the way each member typed it. That replaces reading free-text order notes.
  3. Mark them collected. Add a column to the same sheet, or add a note to each Stripe payment. Either survives a handover better than a WordPress order status nobody else can see.
  4. Refund a mistake in one click in Stripe, with the receipt handled automatically.

What this changes on the site

  • Four plugins come off: WooCommerce, the WooCommerce Stripe gateway, WooCommerce Tax, and Store Vacation.
  • The shopping-cart scripts and Stripe’s library stop loading on every page, including for the many visitors who came to read about a tour.
  • There is no checkout on the club’s own server any more, so the region is no longer the party responsible for securing a payment page.
  • Card fees do not change. Stripe charges the same 2.9% plus 30 cents either way, so this saves volunteer hours rather than money.
  • /shop/, /cart/ and the old product URLs should redirect to the new Regalia page rather than 404, so old links and search results keep working.

What you give up, honestly

A payment link has no basket, no stock levels, no coupon codes, no member-only pricing, and a cap of three custom fields. At two products and roughly one order a week none of that is being used today. If the region ever runs a real catalogue again, that is the moment to reconsider, not now. Two open questions for the board: what PCA pays the region per webstore item, which is published nowhere, and what the treasurer wants to do about sales tax once WooCommerce Tax is removed. Neither is a website decision.

If the board would rather keep one admin screen: Events Manager Pro already takes Stripe and PayPal, so the badge could be sold as a permanently open “ticket”. It is conceptually odd, but it is one fewer system for a single maintainer to remember, and it keeps every payment the region takes in one place.

Three things to change when porting this to WordPress

  1. Swap container queries for media queries. The prototype uses @container so the width buttons in the grey bar actually work. On the real site the page is the viewport, so the same breakpoints become ordinary @media rules. The breakpoint values do not change.
  2. Self-host the two fonts, or drop to system fonts. PCA hosting allows the files. Two self-hosted variable fonts will beat two Google Fonts requests, and a system font stack beats both if the board would rather not think about it.
  3. Wire the status rail to real data. Available places, sold out, and registration-not-yet-open all come from Events Manager fields. Cancelled is best handled as a dedicated event category or a checkbox field, so that marking one event cancelled updates the list, the calendar, and the home page at once.

Real content, and what is not

Event names, dates, prices, venues, capacity figures, news headlines, authors, board names, sponsor names and the club's own description are all taken from the live site as it stood on 25 and 26 August 2026. Anything invented is marked in the interface with a placeholder label: the September membership meeting, the badge batch date, the cancellation policy, sponsorship prices, committee chair names, and the body copy of the sample article.
