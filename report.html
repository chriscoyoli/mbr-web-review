01

Summary for the board

The short version: the website does not need to be rebuilt or moved. It needs three urgent repairs, one honest retirement, and a change in how it is looked after. Almost everything wrong with it is a settings-and-habits problem, not a technology problem.

Start with the good news, because it is genuinely unusual

Monterey Bay takes event bookings and card payments on its own website, with a real headcount limit and a closing date. On the Elkhorn Slough tour, the page shows 27 of 32 seats taken and closes bookings at noon on 19 September. Members pay by card, PayPal, or check, and never leave mby.pca.org until the payment screen.

Every single one of the eight comparable clubs we examined sends its members to somebody else's website to register, usually MotorsportReg or ClubRegistration.net. Golden Gate does not even link its registration system from its own calendar page. This is the one thing Monterey Bay does better than the entire peer group, and the whole plan below is built around not breaking it.

Three things need fixing this month

1. A cancelled event is still being advertised. The 28 August Night at the Races has been cancelled. The cancellation is written in the middle of the event page, but the event still appears as a normal upcoming event on the home page, in the events list, and on the August calendar grid. Anyone who does not open and read the page will believe it is happening. Nothing damages a calendar's credibility faster.

2. The software that takes your members' money is behind on a security update. The plugin that runs event bookings and card payments has a security release outstanding, and it has not been applied. There are 15 updates waiting in total.

3. Only one person can update the website. If Richard Chew is unavailable, nobody publishes an event. This is the single largest risk on this list and the cheapest one to fix. It takes an afternoon.

3.9 screens of scrolling on a phone before a visitor sees a single upcoming event Measured at 390 pixels wide. The heading sits 2,888 pixels down a 6,330 pixel page.
39 store orders in the eight months since the shop opened $1,926 total. Eleven of those orders have still not been marked fulfilled.
28 add-on programs ("plugins") running on the site, all switched on Including three that overlap on security and one diagnostic tool that should not be live.

What is actually wrong, in one paragraph

The home page leads with a photograph and a paragraph of welcome text. Events, which are the club's product, come nearly four phone screens later. There is no "Join" link anywhere in the menu. There is no sponsor page at all, so a business that wants to support the club has nowhere to go. The shop's terms and conditions page still says "Add your terms & conditions here," which is the wording the software ships with, on a site that takes credit cards. The site loads 148 separate files and roughly 7 megabytes of uncompressed material on the home page, including a full copy of the Stripe payment library on a page where nobody is paying for anything. None of this requires new software to fix.

What we recommend

Keep WordPress. Keep the pca.org address. Keep the booking system. Fix what you have. PCA National hosts the site at no charge, so there is no annual bill to reduce by moving, only a new one to create. A rebuild would put the best part of the site at risk for six months and would not touch the two problems that actually matter, which are the cancelled-event kind of mistake and the one-person dependency.

Retire the online shop. Move club clothing to the PCA National region webstore, which prints and ships on demand at no cost or risk to the region and which 77 other regions already use. Sell the name badge through a single Stripe payment link. This removes four programs from the site and all of the card-security responsibility, and at current volumes it costs the club nothing in sales.

Total expected cost: $0 to $1,500 one time, and $100 to $250 a year, most of which the club already spends.

02

Where the brief was wrong

Five assumptions in the brief do not survive contact with the live site. Each one changes the plan.

1. Event registration is not outsourced. It is the site's best feature.

The brief asked which third-party tool is in use. None is. The site runs Events Manager Pro with Stripe and PayPal built in. That is better than every club we benchmarked, and it inverts the usual advice: do not go shopping for a registration vendor, protect the one you have.

2. There are no track events on this calendar.

The brief lists "autocross, DE at Laguna Seca" among the events to promote. Looking at everything published on the site, we found no Driver Education, HPDE, or track day run by Monterey Bay. The About page describes autocross as happening "through Zone 7 series at Salinas Municipal Airport," which is a different organisation's event. The published calendar is tours, concours, dinners, tech sessions, and membership meetings. This matters, because track registration is the complicated part of club websites and Monterey Bay does not have that problem. If the region does run track events off-site, tell us and we will revisit.

3. Annual cost is not the lever the brief assumes.

The brief says "cost per year matters more than one-time polish." True in principle, but PCA National already hosts the site free of charge, so the annual cash cost is close to zero. The scarce resource here is volunteer hours, not dollars. Every recommendation below is priced in hours first.

4. The domain question has an answer, and it is "stay."

PCA National's hosting page confirms regions may use their own domain name at no extra cost. So moving is permitted. It is still the wrong move: it trades free, supported hosting and inherited search authority for a bill and a migration, in exchange for a nicer address. Several good names are unregistered today (montereypca.com, montereybaypca.com, mbrpca.com, mbypca.com, all checked 25 August 2026). If the board wants one, register it defensively for about $12 a year and point it at the existing site. Do not move the site to it.

5. The store is not priority six of seven. It is the thing to remove.

The brief ranks merchandise sixth. The order data says it should not be on the list at all. Details in section 08.

One thing we could not measure, and will not guess

We were unable to obtain Core Web Vitals scores, which are Google's standard speed measurements. Google's public testing service rate-limited every attempt across two days, and running the test from our own environment was blocked by a network proxy. Everything reported under Performance below is a direct measurement of what the page actually loads, taken in a real browser, not a score. Where a number is not verified, this report says so rather than inventing one.

03

The audit, 11 areas

Each area is scored out of five, with the evidence behind the score, how urgent it is, and how much work the fix is. Effort is S under a day, M one to two weeks of evenings, L a month or more. Everything below was checked on 25 and 26 August 2026.

01

Information architecture and navigation

3 / 5HighEffort M

The menu is short and the structure is sane. The problems are that it is organised around the club's org chart rather than what a visitor came to do, and that two of the most valuable destinations are missing or unreachable.

  • There is no "Join" or "Membership" item in the menu at all. The menu is Home, News, Calendar, Photos, Our Club, Zone 7/PCA, Store. Turning Porsche owners into members is priority three in the brief and it has zero navigation surface.
  • The events list page is an orphan. mby.pca.org/events/ is not in the menu. The only way to reach it is a text link partway down the home page. The menu sends you to /calendar/ instead, which is a different page showing the same events in a month grid.
  • Two competing calendar surfaces. /calendar/ is a month grid powered by WP FullCalendar. /events/ is a list powered by Events Manager. They show the same events differently, and neither links to the other.
  • "Zone 7/PCA" is a whole top-level menu that sends people away. Three of its items lead off-site or to pages about other organisations. A visitor's second most prominent choice is "go somewhere else."
  • "Cart" and "Terms & Conditions" are navigation items. These are checkout plumbing sitting in the main menu of a car club, under Store, alongside Shop.
  • The Montereyan sits under Our Club as though it were current. It is a PDF archive that ended in 1997, with two revival issues in 2009. mby.pca.org/montereyan/
VerdictNothing here is broken, but the menu answers "who are we" when visitors are asking "what is on, and how do I get in." Rewriting it is a one-evening job.
02

Content quality and freshness

2 / 5CriticalEffort S

The publishing rhythm is genuinely good. The accuracy is not. That combination is worse than a quiet site, because members learn that a current-looking page can still be wrong.

  • A cancelled event is presented as upcoming. The 28 August Night at the Races page opens with "It is with great disappointment that I have to announce our Night at the Races event is cancelled," yet the event appears normally on the home page, in the events list, and on the August calendar grid, with no cancelled marking anywhere. mby.pca.org/events/2026-night-at-the-races/
  • The shop's terms page is still the factory placeholder. It reads, in full, "Add your terms & conditions here." This is on a site that accepts credit cards. mby.pca.org/terms-conditions/
  • A product name is misspelled on the live store. The name badge is listed as "Moneterey Bay - Name Badge."
  • Events are inconsistently complete. Elkhorn Slough on 19 September has a price, a headcount, a deadline, and a booking form. Oktoberfest on 11 October has a date, a venue, and the words "Save the Date," with no price and no way to register.
  • Personal contact details are published in plain text. The president's personal Gmail address and personal mobile number appear on event pages. That is a spam magnet and it makes the role impossible to hand over.
  • The board page carries no way to contact the board. Four officers are named, with no email, no photographs, and no committee chairs. Below them is a list of past presidents back to 1961. mby.pca.org/club-leadership/

Credit where it is due: the news stream is genuinely active, with posts on 19 August, 15 August, and 24 July 2026 across 22 pages of archive, authored by Richard Chew and Vega Roecker. Photo albums are current to 12 August 2026. Very few volunteer sites keep that cadence.

VerdictScore held at 2 despite the good cadence, because a calendar that advertises a cancelled event is not a calendar anyone can rely on. Every item above is fixable in under a day.

04

Mobile experience at 390 pixels

2 / 5HighEffort M

Measured in a real browser at a 390 pixel viewport, the width of a current iPhone.

  • The home page is 6,330 pixels tall. That is roughly 8.5 phone screens.
  • "Upcoming Events" begins 2,888 pixels down, which is 3.9 screens of scrolling. "In the News" is at 4,016 pixels, or 5.4 screens. The "Join Us" button is at 1,054 pixels.
  • The header consumes about 40 percent of the first screen before any content. A rotating banner photograph is followed by a tall blue band holding the crest and a script wordmark, then the cart and menu buttons.
  • 33 tap targets are smaller than 24 × 24 pixels. WCAG 2.2 sets 24 pixels as the AA minimum. WCAG is the international accessibility standard.
  • Good news: nothing overflows sideways. The underlying theme is built on Bootstrap and the responsive grid holds cleanly from 390 pixels upward. There is no horizontal scrolling and no broken table reflow.
VerdictThe technical responsiveness is fine. The editorial priority is wrong. Assume most visitors are on a phone and they are being shown a photograph and a paragraph of history where the next three events should be.
05

Performance

2 / 5HighEffort M

Core Web Vitals: not verified. Google's public scoring service rate-limited every attempt and an independent test run was blocked by a network proxy. What follows is measured directly from the page in a browser, which is evidence of what the site does, not a score of how Google grades it.

  • 148 separate files on the home page, totalling roughly 7.2 megabytes uncompressed. That includes 58 JavaScript files and 22 stylesheets.
  • Stripe's payment library loads on the home page, where nobody is paying for anything. So do the WooCommerce shopping-cart scripts, on every page of the site.
  • jQuery plus thirteen separate jQuery UI modules load site-wide, along with Moment.js, FullCalendar, Fancybox, Font Awesome, and Bootstrap.
  • React and ReactDOM load on the public home page. These are part of WordPress's block editor and are heavy for a page that is not an editor.
  • The banner image is 1,200 × 400 pixels displayed at 1,470 × 490. It is being stretched beyond its own resolution on any normal laptop, and much further on a high-resolution display.
  • No caching plugin, no content delivery network, and no modern image formats. Images are JPEG and PNG only. There is no WebP or AVIF conversion, which typically saves 25 to 50 percent of image weight for free.
  • What is already right: 22 images use lazy loading and 27 carry responsive image sets, both WordPress defaults working as intended.
VerdictRoughly half of what the home page downloads is there because a plugin loads itself everywhere rather than only where it is needed. A caching plugin plus turning off the shopping cart scripts would be the single largest speed win available, and both are configuration, not code.
06

Accessibility, WCAG 2.2 AA

2 / 5HighEffort M

Measured on the home page and the calendar page. Colour contrast was not machine-tested and is marked unverified.

  • Two H1 headings on the home page, one of them empty. A page should have exactly one main heading. Screen readers announce the empty one as a heading with no text.
  • Only five headings on the entire home page. Event titles, news items, and sponsors are not headings, so a screen reader user cannot jump between sections.
  • 22 of 78 links have no accessible name. That is 28 percent of the links on the page announcing as "link" with nothing after it. This fails WCAG 2.4.4 and 4.1.2.
  • 33 tap targets below the 24 pixel minimum, failing WCAG 2.2 criterion 2.5.8.
  • The calendar page has no H1 at all. Its first heading is an H2 reading "August 2026." mby.pca.org/calendar/
  • One form field with no label. 21 images carry an empty alt attribute, which is correct for decoration but wrong if any of them are content.
  • What is already right: the page language is declared correctly, every image has an alt attribute present, and the semantic landmarks (header, nav, main, footer) exist.
  • Not verified: colour contrast ratios, keyboard tab order, and focus visibility were not systematically tested.
VerdictNothing here needs a rebuild. The empty H1, the unnamed links, and the tap-target sizes are theme and content fixes. Do them while the homepage is being reworked, not as a separate project.
07

SEO and local discovery

2 / 5MediumEffort M
  • No search optimisation plugin is installed. The sitemap at /wp-sitemap.xml is the plain one WordPress generates by itself, and there is no Yoast or Rank Math in the plugin list. That means no control over page titles, no meta descriptions, and no structured data unless a plugin adds it.
  • The calendar page has no meta description. Google writes its own snippet instead.
  • Event structured data not verified. Events Manager can publish Event schema, which is what makes an event eligible for Google's rich event results. Whether it is switched on was not confirmed.
  • The robots file is the WooCommerce default, blocking log and upload folders. Nothing is wrongly blocked.
  • The subdomain question, plainly. Living at mby.pca.org means the site inherits some of pca.org's search authority for free, which is a real advantage a new domain would take years to earn. The cost is that the region can never build its own brand in search, and it competes for attention with roughly 145 sibling region subdomains. The advantage outweighs the cost at this club's size. Keep the address.
  • There is demonstrable organic search traffic. One store order in the admin is attributed to "Organic: Google," so the site is being found.
VerdictInstalling one free SEO plugin and writing descriptions for the twelve pages that matter is a weekend of work and the highest-return item in this section.
08

WordPress health

2 / 5CriticalEffort M

Verified directly from the site's own admin screens. The server itself is in good shape: PHP 8.4, WooCommerce 10.9.4, both current. The problem is what has accumulated on top.

  • The bookings and payments plugin has a security release outstanding. It has not been applied. Nothing else on this page is more urgent. Specifics are held back in this public edition.
  • 28 plugins, all active, with 15 updates waiting across 12 plugins plus core.
  • Three overlapping security plugins are running at once. Two firewalls on one site is a common cause of odd, hard-to-diagnose breakage.
  • A server diagnostic tool is live in production. It exists to dump the full server configuration. It sits behind the admin login, but it should not be installed on a live site at all.
  • Three more maintenance and migration tools left switched on. These are things you turn on for an afternoon, not leave running for months.
  • Two overlapping gallery and lightbox tools: Envira Gallery and Firelight Lightbox. Envira is active but the site's photo albums are all hosted on Google Photos, so it may be doing nothing at all.
  • Backups run on BackUpWordPress. Confirm this plugin is still actively maintained before relying on it, and confirm where the backups are actually stored and when one was last restored successfully. A backup nobody has tested is not a backup.
  • The public contact form has no visible spam protection. The captcha plugin that is installed covers a different form.
VerdictThis is the section that most argues against a rebuild. None of these problems is caused by the platform. All of them are caused by nobody having a maintenance routine. A new site would accumulate exactly the same list.

10

Engagement surface

3 / 5MediumEffort M
  • Email is handled well and off-site. The region uses PCA National's Emailer, which is free with the charter and means the club does not hold its own copy of members' email addresses. That is the right call for privacy and the right call for effort. Keep it.
  • There is no current newsletter. The Montereyan ended in 1997. What exists today is a news stream on the website plus emails through the Emailer. That is a defensible modern setup, but the site presents a dead 1997 archive as though it were a club feature.
  • Photo albums are entirely on Google Photos. Ten albums for 2026, most recent 12 August. Zero maintenance burden, which is good, but the photos do nothing for search visibility and visitors leave the site to see them. Meanwhile Envira Gallery is installed and active on the site, apparently unused.
  • No member directory and no classifieds, correctly. Membership records live with PCA National and PCA's own Mart handles classifieds. Both are linked. Do not rebuild either.
  • Privacy is mixed. Event booking forms include a data-consent checkbox, which is good practice. Against that, an officer's personal email and mobile number are published in plain text.
  • Sponsors get logo placement and nothing else. Five sponsors, sidebar and footer logos, links straight out. No tiers, no descriptions, no member discounts, no page of their own.
VerdictScored 3 because what exists is active and the outsourcing decisions are mostly correct. The gap is that none of it is being used to give members a reason to come back to the site.
11

Governance and cost of ownership

1 / 5CriticalEffort S

This is the lowest score in the report and the cheapest thing to fix.

  • One person can maintain this website. Confirmed by the board. Richard Chew and Vega Roecker both appear as post authors, so at least two people can publish news, but effectively one person owns the site. If that person is unavailable for a month, events do not get published and the security update does not get applied.
  • Cash cost is close to zero and that is the point. Hosting is provided free by PCA National. The only recurring cash costs we can identify are card processing fees, roughly $100 a year at current volume, and any paid plugin licences, chiefly Events Manager Pro and possibly Envira Gallery, both unverified.
  • The real cost is volunteer attention, and it is being spent badly. Every event currently touches up to four systems: an Events Manager entry, a separate news post, a Google Photos album afterwards, and the PCA Emailer. Nothing is wrong with that, but nobody has written it down.
  • Time to publish a new event: not verified. We could not measure this without watching someone do it. It is worth timing once, because it is the number that determines whether a second volunteer will stick.
  • PCA National's support is thinner than it looks. National's hosting page states plainly that PCA "provides support for hosting and related issues, but not website programming or design," and the entire PCA Website Committee is one person serving roughly 145 regions. Do not plan around National rescuing the site.
VerdictAdd a second administrator and write a one-page "how to publish an event" note. That single afternoon does more for the club's long-term web position than any redesign in this report.

04

How eight peers compare

Eight comparators, all checked 25 August 2026. Selected to cover the three groups that matter: the regional flagship, the Zone 7 neighbours competing for the same members, large regions that have solved problems at scale, and one club outside PCA chosen purely for design quality.

Why these eight

  • Golden Gate is the flagship Northern California region and the obvious quality benchmark.
  • Loma Prieta and Diablo are Zone 7 neighbours. Members and event attendance move between these clubs and Monterey Bay.
  • Potomac is the largest PCA region, Maverick the largest in Texas, and Rocky Mountain runs one of the densest event calendars in the club. All three have already hit problems Monterey Bay has not yet reached.
  • Pacific Northwest is the only one running a managed club platform end to end, which is exactly the Option C question made concrete.
  • BMW CCA Puget Sound is the non-PCA reference. It has the best-designed and best-built site in the whole set, and it is instructive precisely because Monterey Bay should not copy its architecture.

A cautionary ninth, worth thirty seconds

Chicago Region (pca-chicago.org) is a perfectly reasonable WordPress site whose home page, as served today, still advertises its next events as the Annual Awards Dinner on 10 December 2022 and a Chili Tasting in February 2023. The region is demonstrably active; the home page simply stopped being maintained. This is what a good site looks like three years after a redesign with no maintenance routine attached. It is the single strongest argument in this report against spending money on a rebuild.

What each does better, and what Monterey Bay already does better

Golden Gate

Better: a genuine brand system carried consistently across every page; a tiered sponsor showcase on the home page rather than a logo strip in the footer; live autocross timing pushed to competitors' phones during the event.

Monterey Bay does better: a member can actually register and pay on the club's own website. Golden Gate's registration system is not linked from its calendar, its track page, or its autocross page.

Loma Prieta

Better: the home page news list marks events SOLD OUT, which is the only capacity signal anywhere in the peer set; the newsletter is current and readable in the browser with archives to 2004; a Lands' End store lets members put the club logo on anything with no inventory risk to the club.

Monterey Bay does better: one events system instead of three. Loma Prieta runs a Google Calendar embed, a curated home page list, and an abandoned blog last updated April 2024, all at once.

Diablo

Better: a weekly dated post that is simultaneously the email, the news archive, and the place every registration link lives; an email signup form on the site; the clearest explanation of track-day onboarding in the group.

Monterey Bay does better: Diablo's own event pages are calendar embeds with no way to sign up. Monterey Bay's event pages take the booking.

Potomac

Better: events sit above the fold on the home page, before any welcome copy; members can subscribe to a calendar feed for just the event types they care about; a four-item quick-links strip puts the most common member tasks one click from the home page.

Monterey Bay does better: Potomac's sponsor page is an alphabetical list of plain text links with an explicit non-endorsement disclaimer. Monterey Bay's five sponsors at least get logos and prominence, which is a better foundation to build a real programme on.

Maverick

Better: by far the best sponsor programme in the set, with three named tiers, logos, full contact details and social links for each sponsor, and home page placement for the top tier; dedicated volunteer signup pages instead of a mailto link; named specialist volunteer roles published so people know what they are signing up for.

Monterey Bay does better: Maverick's blog has been dormant since September 2025 and its headline membership figure is dated March 2024. Monterey Bay publishes news most months.

Rocky Mountain

Better: a downloadable library for event organisers, with an event chair manual, budget spreadsheets, and a timeline checklist, so knowledge survives volunteer turnover; plain-English explainers for every event type; payment deferred until shortly before the event, which lowers the commitment barrier at signup.

Monterey Bay does better: Rocky Mountain's news posts display no dates at all, and its newsletter index is out of step with the PDFs actually published. Monterey Bay dates everything.

Pacific Northwest

Better: the best calendar in the set, with month, week, day and list views, keyword search, roughly thirty category filters, and a toggle for "only show events with spaces left"; native member login and member-only pricing with no plugins to maintain; menu labels written in club language, "At the Track" and "In the Garage" rather than CMS page names.

Monterey Bay does better: one registration system rather than three. A Pacific Northwest member has to learn a different signup process depending on whether the event is social, track, or autocross.

BMW CCA Puget Sound

Better: the event page sells the event before it transacts, with a proper hero image, a narrative, a scannable facts box, a track map, and related events, and only then hands off to register; the newsletter is individual web articles with bylines and categories rather than a PDF, so fifty years of club writing works as searchable content; sponsorship is expressed as a member benefit, with each partner's actual discount printed next to their name.

Monterey Bay does better: a volunteer can update it. Puget Sound's site is a developer-built static site on an ageing framework. If their one technical volunteer leaves, that site cannot be edited by anyone on the board.

05

Patterns worth stealing

Ten moves the strongest sites share, sorted honestly into the ones that are genuinely valuable and the ones that are just expensive.

Worth doing

  1. Put the next three events above the fold. (Potomac) A visitor should see what is happening next before they see a welcome paragraph. This is the single highest-value change on the list and it costs nothing but a decision.
  2. Show capacity state on every event. (Loma Prieta) "5 spaces left" and "SOLD OUT" both create urgency and prevent disappointment. Monterey Bay already stores this data in Events Manager Pro and simply does not display it in the listings.
  3. Mark cancelled events as cancelled, visibly, everywhere they appear. (nobody does this well, which is the opportunity) A struck-through title and a red "Cancelled" badge in the list, the grid, and the home page.
  4. One dated weekly or fortnightly post that carries every link. (Diablo) It becomes the email, the news archive, and the registration hub at the same time. One piece of work, three outputs. This is the highest-leverage editorial habit in the entire peer set.
  5. Give sponsors a page with tiers and member discounts. (Maverick, BMW Puget Sound) Printing each sponsor's actual member discount turns a logo wall into both a retention benefit and a sellable sponsorship product. Monterey Bay currently has no sponsor page at all.
  6. Calendar subscription feeds by event type. (Potomac) A member subscribes once and every tour appears in their own phone calendar forever. Cheap, and it makes the site useful without anyone visiting it.
  7. Answer "what do you actually do" before "what is on Tuesday." (BMW CCA National Capital) A short row of activity tiles, tours, concours, socials, tech sessions, converts a curious Porsche owner far better than a paragraph of history.
  8. Write the event page so it sells before it transacts. (BMW Puget Sound) Photograph, a paragraph of what the day is actually like, a facts box with price and deadline, a map, then the booking form. Monterey Bay has the booking form already and needs the rest.
  9. Keep an organiser document library. (Rocky Mountain) Event chair checklist, budget template, timeline. This is how a volunteer club survives turnover, and it is a folder of documents.
  10. Publish role email addresses, not personal ones. (most of the set) president@, membership@, sponsors@, webmaster@. It protects individuals from spam and makes every handover trivial.

Expensive, and not worth it here

  • A native mobile app. (Maverick) It duplicates the website, needs two app store accounts, and needs a developer forever. A well-built mobile web page does the same job.
  • A gated members' forum. (Maverick) Club forums almost always go quiet, and the club already has a Facebook presence doing this work for free.
  • A developer-built static site. (BMW Puget Sound) Beautiful, fast, and unmaintainable by a board. For a club with one technical volunteer this is a serious risk dressed as an upgrade.
  • Live event timing. (Golden Gate, Loma Prieta) Only pays off if the club runs its own autocross programme. Monterey Bay's autocross runs through the Zone 7 series.
  • A printed newsletter. (Rocky Mountain, 1,800 households) Real money, real deadlines, real volunteer burnout.
  • Custom classifieds. (Rocky Mountain) PCA's national Mart already exists and is already linked.
06

Three options, honestly costed

All figures are USD ranges. One-time costs assume paid help where a volunteer cannot reasonably do the work; where the club has a capable volunteer, that line goes to zero and the cost becomes hours. Annual figures exclude hosting, which PCA National provides free in every option except C.

Option A

Optimise in place

Recommended

Keep WordPress, the bootscore theme, the pca.org address, and the Events Manager Pro booking system. Rebuild the home page layout inside the existing theme, cut the plugin count roughly in half, fix the content defects, add caching and search optimisation, retire the shop, and put a maintenance routine in place.

One-time
$0 to $1,500. Zero if volunteers do it. The paid portion, if any, is a designer or developer for the home page template, the accessibility pass, and dequeuing the site-wide scripts, roughly 12 to 20 hours.
Annual
$100 to $250. Card processing fees around $100, plus Events Manager Pro licence renewal and one optional caching or image plugin. Hosting stays $0.
Volunteer skill
Unchanged. Anyone who can already publish a WordPress post can keep running it. No new tool to learn.
Migration risk
Very low. Nothing moves. No content migration, no URL changes, no downtime, no re-testing of payments.
Fixes
Every Critical and High finding in this report except the theme's design ceiling.
Does not fix
The site will still look like a competent Bootstrap theme, not like a designed brand. No design system, ordinary typography. See the recommendation for why we accept that.
Time to launch
30 to 90 days, incrementally. Nothing waits for a launch date, and the urgent items land in week one.
Option B

Rebuild on modern WordPress

Viable, premature

Stay on WordPress and stay on PCA hosting, but replace the theme with either a block theme or one well-supported lightweight theme, rebuild the page templates and navigation from scratch, curate the plugin set down to essentials, and carry Events Manager Pro across.

One-time
$3,500 to $9,000 contracted, or roughly 80 to 150 volunteer hours. The range depends on whether the club buys a design or adapts a commercial theme.
Annual
$200 to $350. Card fees, theme licence, plugin licences. Hosting stays $0.
Volunteer skill
Higher during the build, and different afterwards. If the new theme uses the block editor, the one person who currently maintains the site has to relearn how to lay out a page. That is a real cost for a club with a single maintainer.
Migration risk
Medium to high. The risky part is not the 215 news posts, it is Events Manager Pro's booking templates and payment configuration. That is the one thing on this site that works better than every peer, and a rebuild puts it on the table.
Fixes
Everything A fixes, plus a proper design system and full template control.
Does not fix
The single-maintainer problem, which gets worse for three to six months. PCA's hosting constraints, which are FTP access only with no command line. And the underlying issue, which is that nobody has a maintenance habit.
Time to launch
3 to 6 months, with a cutover date and a testing window.
Option C

Replatform

Not advised

Three real variants, all of which mean leaving WordPress.

  • C1, a managed club platform such as ClubExpress, the way Pacific Northwest runs. Membership, calendar, registration, store, and member login all in one subscription.
  • C2, Squarespace plus hosted services, the way Golden Gate and Diablo run. Squarespace for pages, MotorsportReg or Eventbrite for registration, the PCA webstore for merchandise.
  • C3, a modern static or hybrid build with hosted services, the way BMW Puget Sound runs.
One-time
$2,000 to $12,000. C1 is cheapest to stand up, C3 the most expensive.
Annual
$0 to $1,200+. C1 and C2 both carry a subscription and both mean giving up PCA National's free hosting. ClubExpress and Squarespace pricing were not verified for this report and must be quoted directly before any decision. C3 can host for $0 on Cloudflare Pages.
Volunteer skill
C1 low, which is its entire selling point. C2 low. C3 developer only, permanently.
Migration risk
High in all three. C2 and C3 both destroy the on-site booking system and force a move to MotorsportReg or Eventbrite, which means every registration leaves the club's website. C1 replaces it with ClubExpress's own system, which is capable but is a full re-training exercise for members as well as volunteers.
Fixes
C1 genuinely does fix the maintenance problem, because the vendor maintains the platform. That is not nothing.
Does not fix
It creates a bill where there was none, gives up inherited search authority, and trades a known system for vendor dependence.
Time to launch
4 to 9 months.
07

Recommendation

Take Option A. Do not rebuild and do not move.

Spend nothing to a few hundred dollars, work through the ninety-day list, and put a maintenance routine in place. Revisit Option B in two years only if the club has by then proved it can keep three people publishing to the site. If it cannot, a rebuild will decay exactly the way Chicago Region's did.

Why, in five arguments

  1. There is no annual bill to reduce, only one to create. PCA National hosts the site free. Options B and C cannot save money because the money is not being spent. Every replatforming case study that starts with "we cut hosting costs" does not apply here.
  2. Monterey Bay already has the best event registration in the peer group and both alternatives put it at risk. Eight comparable clubs, including the flagship, all send members off-site to register. This club does not. That is a competitive advantage in filling events, which is priority one in the brief. Rebuilding risks it. Replatforming to Squarespace or a static site destroys it outright.
  3. Nine of the eleven audit failures are configuration and editorial, not architecture. A cancelled event still showing as upcoming, a placeholder terms page, a missing sponsor page, three security plugins fighting each other, no caching. New paint does not change any of that. Chicago Region proves the point: a perfectly good WordPress site advertising December 2022 events three and a half years later.
  4. The real risk is a single maintainer, and a rebuild makes it worse before it makes it better. For three to six months during a rebuild, the number of people who understand the site goes down, not up. Adding a second administrator and writing a one-page runbook fixes the actual risk this week, for nothing.
  5. The club's constraint is volunteer attention, and Option A is the only one that spends it on outcomes. Every hour spent choosing a theme is an hour not spent writing the weekly post that fills the next tour.

Where we are trading polish for sustainability, said plainly

Option A will not produce a site that looks like BMW Puget Sound's. The typography will stay ordinary. There will be no considered brand system, no bespoke event page layout, no beautiful photography treatment. A designer looking at the result in six months will call it a tidy Bootstrap site, and they will be right.

We are choosing that deliberately. A striking site that one person can maintain is worth less to this club than a plain site that three people can.

The working prototype shows how far the presentation can be pushed within Option A, using the theme and tools already installed. Eleven screens, real content from this site, and a build note mapping each screen to a WordPress template. It is deliberately built to be achievable, not to be impressive. On it, the next event sits on the first screen at 390, 768 and 1440 pixels wide, against 3.9 screens of scrolling today.

On the domain name

PCA National permits regions to use their own domain. Four good names were unregistered when checked on 25 August 2026: montereypca.com, montereybaypca.com, mbrpca.com, and mbypca.com. If the board wants one, montereypca.com is the strongest: short, sayable on a phone call, and unambiguous.

Register it for roughly $12 a year and point it at the existing site so it works in print and on flyers. Do not migrate the site to it. Moving away from mby.pca.org gives up inherited search authority and free hosting in exchange for a nicer address, which is a bad trade at this club's size. Confirm any redirect arrangement with the PCA Region Website Chair first, since PCA controls the DNS for the current address.

08

The store decision

This is a standalone recommendation, separable from everything else in the report.

Recommendation: retire WooCommerce. Split the store in two.

Clothing and general merchandise move to a PCA National region webstore at pcawebstore.org. National prints on demand in Columbia, Maryland, ships direct, and carries all the inventory. The region holds no stock, fronts no money, and does no fulfilment. Seventy-seven regions already have one, including Golden Gate, Loma Prieta, Diablo, Potomac, Maverick, and Rocky Mountain. Monterey Bay does not. Email webstore@national.pca.org to open one.

The name badge, which is club-specific and nobody else can make, moves to a single Stripe payment link with a "Name as it should appear" field built in. Stripe hosts the page, takes the same fee it takes today, and no plugin is required.

The umbrella is discontinued, or moved to the webstore if National carries an equivalent.

The break-even, and why there is not one

The usual question is "how many units a year justify a store." That question does not apply here, and it is worth being precise about why.

Read the bottom row, not the top

The margin line is identical on both paths, because a Stripe payment link charges the same fee and the PCA webstore does the printing either way. So there is no volume at which WooCommerce starts to pay for itself, because WooCommerce is not producing any revenue the alternative does not also produce. It is producing about 21 extra volunteer hours a year, a checkout page the club is responsible for securing, and a set of shopping-cart scripts that load on every page of the site for every visitor.

WooCommerce would begin to earn its keep at roughly 8 to 10 products with sizes and colours, plus about 250 orders a year, which is around five orders a week, because that is the point at which a real cart, stock levels, and member pricing save more time than they cost. Monterey Bay is running about one order a week, with two products, one of which is on backorder.

Why not print-on-demand, which is otherwise the obvious answer

Printful, Bonfire, and similar services are cheap, good, and would work well for most clubs. They are the wrong answer here for a trademark reason rather than a commercial one.

PCA's Intellectual Property Policy grants each region a licence to use PCA marks "in conjunction with its operation and activities and for no other purpose whatsoever," and states a region may not "assign or sublicense any of the Trademarks... unless such permission is granted by PCA in writing." Separately, the Porsche crest and the word Porsche are registered trademarks of Dr. Ing. h.c. F. Porsche AG, not of PCA, so PCA is not even the ultimate owner of the most valuable mark on the merchandise.

Sending club artwork to a commercial print-on-demand vendor is, on the face of it, exactly the sublicensing the policy restricts. The PCA webstore sidesteps the entire question, because PCA is the printer. That is a better argument for the webstore than any margin comparison, and it holds regardless of what National's revenue share turns out to be.

The one number to go and get

What a region actually earns from a PCA webstore is not published anywhere. We checked the webstore, six region pages that link to it, and PCA's own documents. Email webstore@national.pca.org and ask three questions: what does the region earn per item, is there any setup cost or minimum, and how are region logos supplied and approved. If the answer is "nothing," the apparel decision is still right, because the alternative today is no apparel at all.

Sources, assumptions, open questions

Assumptions

  • Hosting is provided free by PCA National. Confirmed by the board, consistent with PCA's published hosting page. Every cost figure in this report depends on it.
  • Merchandise volume is 25 to 100 items a year. Confirmed by the board and corroborated by the order data, which shows 39 orders in eight months.
  • Effectively one person maintains the site. Confirmed by the board. Two names appear as post authors.
  • No one-time budget has been agreed. All three options are therefore priced with a volunteer-only path as well as a paid path.
  • Net margin of $7 per item is assumed in the store table. The club's actual cost per badge was not available. The conclusion does not depend on the figure.
  • Volunteer time valued at nothing in cash, but treated as the scarcest resource in every recommendation.
  • Traffic and analytics were not reviewed. MonsterInsights is installed, so Google Analytics data exists, but we did not access it. No traffic figure appears anywhere in this report.

Explicitly not verified

  • Core Web Vitals and Lighthouse scores, for mby.pca.org and for all eight comparators. Google's public service rate-limited every attempt over two days and an independent run was blocked by a network proxy. No score is quoted anywhere.
  • Colour contrast ratios, keyboard tab order, and focus visibility. Not systematically tested.
  • Whether Events Manager is emitting Event structured data.
  • Region economics of the PCA webstore. Margin, royalty, setup cost, and minimums are published nowhere.
  • Current price of an Events Manager Pro licence, and of Envira Gallery if licensed.
  • ClubRegistration.net pricing, which the vendor does not publish, and current ClubExpress and Squarespace pricing.
  • PCA's actual current server stack. The published hosting page describes Windows Server 2008, CentOS 6, PHP 5.3 and MySQL 5.5, which is contradicted by the site itself running PHP 8.4. The page is stale; the real stack is unknown.
  • Whether BackUpWordPress is still maintained, and where backups are stored.
  • Time for a volunteer to publish a new event. Not measurable without observing it.
  • Region Procedures Manual sections 5.2 and 5.3 (Region Website, On-line Event Registration), pages 128 to 133. Repeated extraction attempts on the PDF failed. Someone should read those six pages directly before the board votes.

Open questions for the board

  1. Does the region run any track or Driver Education event? We found none published.
  2. Who besides Richard Chew should hold administrator access, and who is the backup?
  3. Is there an existing sponsor rate card, even informally, that a sponsor page should reflect?
  4. What does the region actually pay per name badge and umbrella?
  5. Does the board want a memorable domain name for print and word of mouth, given that the site itself should stay where it is?
  6. Has anyone confirmed a backup can actually be restored?
